In **Over time**, after the [charts of the server](/docs/getting-started/dashboard/server-charts), the **Requests** part shows what the <Term id="proxy" /> answered for your services. Use it to find a deploy that fails or a service that is slow.

## Read the charts

| Chart | Content |
|---|---|
| **Requests** | The requests per second to your services, by <Term id="status-code" />: **2xx**, **3xx**, **4xx** and **5xx**. When a deploy starts to fail, the **5xx** part jumps. |
| **Latency** | The <Term id="latency" /> of the requests, as two <Term id="percentile">percentiles</Term>. **p50**: half the requests were faster. **p95**: 95% of the requests were faster. |

The charts do not show the requests to the dashboard or the unrouted requests. Put the pointer on a chart to see the values of a step.

## Read the table

| Column | Content |
|---|---|
| **Name** | The service. Click it to open its page. |
| **Requests** | The requests per second, on average over the range, and their number |
| **Error rate** | The part of the requests with a 5xx status code, in percent |
| **p95** | 95% of the requests took this time or less. |

The busiest service comes first. Then come two more rows:

| Row | Requests |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | The requests to the <Term id="hostname" /> of the dashboard |
| **Unrouted** | The requests to a hostname that no service has, for example from bots. They do not change the numbers of your services. |

<Accordions>
  <Accordion title="No requests yet">
    Until the proxy answers a request, the part shows **No requests yet**. If only the dashboard or unrouted hostnames got requests in the range, the charts show **No request to your services in this range**.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What the proxy counts">
    The proxy counts each request one time, over HTTP and HTTPS. The time stops when the headers of the answer go out, thus a long download does not change it. The `502` and `503` pages of the proxy are 5xx. A <Term id="redirect" /> from HTTP to HTTPS is a 3xx of the service. A <Term id="websocket" /> counts one time, as a 2xx, and is not in the latency. If the client goes away before the answer, the request is a 4xx (`499`) with the time that the client waited. Thus a service that does not answer shows as slow.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="When Ferry stops">
    The proxy keeps the counts in memory. Every 10 seconds, and when it stops, ferryd writes the new counts to `ferry.db`. A stop loses only the requests that end while the proxy closes its connections: 10 seconds at most.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="The size of the history">
    With 20 busy services, 24 hours of requests use about 18 MB of `ferry.db`. Ferry deletes them after 24 hours, with the other readings.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="API">
    The charts and the table read the fields `requests` and `request_targets` of `GET /api/v1/server/metrics/history`. The last step can cover less time than the others: its field `seconds` tells how much. The field `requests` of `GET /api/v1/server/metrics` has the requests of the last 10 seconds. See [Server metrics](/docs/reference/api/server).
  </Accordion>
</Accordions>
